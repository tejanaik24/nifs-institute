Chapter 01

Introduction: what is ergonomics?

Ergonomics is the field of study concerned with keeping people safe, comfortable and productive by accommodating human characteristics, capabilities and limitations in a product or process, at work and at home.

Rather than making the person adapt to fit the task, ergonomics fits the task to the person.

Also called human factors engineering, it is the scientific discipline of designing tools, tasks, environments and systems to fit the capabilities and limitations of people. It aims to optimise human well-being and system performance by reducing work-related musculoskeletal disorders (WMSDs), fatigue and discomfort, while maximising efficiency and productivity.

Chapter 06

Musculoskeletal disorders (MSDs)

MSDs affect muscles, bones, joints, ligaments and tendons. Symptoms range from mild aches to severe issues, may worsen with activity or improve with rest, and often become persistent if ignored.

PainAching, throbbing, sharp or dull pain, sometimes constant, sometimes intermittent.
StiffnessEspecially after rest or on waking, limiting movement.
WeaknessLoss of strength or increased muscle fatigue.
SwellingInflammation around joints or tissues, sometimes with warmth or redness.
Reduced range of motionDifficulty bending or moving joints fully.
Joint noisesClicking, popping or grinding sounds (crepitus).
Numbness / tingling"Pins and needles", burning, or body parts "falling asleep".
TendernessPain when a specific muscle or joint is touched.
FatigueFeeling tired or lacking energy in the affected areas.

Notice it early. Report symptoms before they turn into a serious, lost-time injury.

Chapter 07

Key ergonomic principles

Design work to fit the user: neutral postures, less force and motion, everything in the comfort zone.

Awkward. The person goes to the task.

Neutral. The task comes to the person, inside the power zone.

01

Maintain neutral postures

Work in natural, relaxed positions. Avoid awkward wrist, elbow, shoulder or back angles.

02

Work in the power zone

Keep frequently used tools and items within easy reach, between mid-thigh and shoulder height, to minimise reaching.

03

Reduce excessive force

Lighten loads, use larger muscles and mechanical assists, or ask for help to avoid straining.

04

Minimise excessive motion

Optimise tasks to reduce repetitive movements, which can cause conditions like tendinitis.

05

Minimise static load

Avoid holding one position for long. Use jigs or clamps to reduce manual positioning.

06

Minimise contact stress

Avoid hard or sharp surfaces pressing on the body, which can restrict nerves and blood flow.

07

Allow movement and stretching

Build in breaks, exercise and position changes to reduce fatigue and improve muscle balance.

08

Provide adequate clearance

Leave enough space under desks and around workstations for proper leg and body positioning.

09

Keep a comfortable environment

Control lighting (avoid glare), temperature, noise and vibration.
